\section*{Purpose and measurement stage}
The diagnostic compares two spatial regions within each individual flat
exposure: \texttt{near\_readout} (numerator) and \texttt{parallel\_far}
(denominator). Their configured active-coordinate rectangles share the same
$x$ range and differ in $y$. It examines the observed readout-side bifurcation;
it does not estimate a shutter coefficient or fit a detector response.

The current input is the retained raw-pair ASDF archive, bias-subtracted
\emph{without overscan correction}. This stage is propagated into every ratio
product and displayed in HTML. The owner requires a subsequent consistent
overscan subtraction for all bias and flat frames before interpretation of
corrected linearity. That measurement pass is separate: no images are read,
no previous measurements replaced, and no line fit recomputed here.

\section*{Identity and equations}
For each detector, match samples by original input index, asset name, HDU,
pair identifier and pair member. Require equal exposure time and detector.
Time alone is not an identity: the two pair members remain separate. Duplicate
identities or unequal ROI populations are rejected. With finite positive
bias-subtracted ROI signals $N_j,D_j$ (ADU), form
\begin{equation}
R_j=\frac{N_j}{D_j}.
\end{equation}
Missing, nonfinite or nonpositive signals yield an unavailable ratio while
preserving the input samples and reason. Let the inclusive reference bounds be
$L=15000$ and $U=35000$ ADU, explicitly recorded with the product. Define
\begin{align}
\mathcal J&=\{j:R_j\text{ available},\ L\le N_j\le U,\ L\le D_j\le U\},\\
R_{\rm ref}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal J|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}R_j,\\
Q_j&=R_j/R_{\rm ref},\qquad P_j=100(Q_j-1).
\end{align}
The reference is the unweighted mean of individual ratios, not a ratio of
averaged signals. Save its value, count and exact member identities. If the
reference is unavailable, keep raw ratios and leave $Q_j,P_j$ unavailable.
All exposures, including those outside the reference bounds, remain retained.

\section*{Interpretation and limits}
In a linear detector with a stable illumination pattern, write
$N_j=k_N F_j T_j$ and $D_j=k_D F_j T_j$, where $T_j$ is effective exposure time
and $F_j$ is common illumination. Then $R_j=k_N/k_D$: both common brightness
and common timing cancel. The normalization removes the constant spatial
response ratio without applying a flat-field correction to the measurements.

Residual additive terms do not cancel:
\begin{equation}
R_j=\frac{k_N F_j T_j+B_{N,j}}{k_D F_j T_j+B_{D,j}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently an overscan or bias mismatch can be particularly conspicuous at
low signal. Changing illumination geometry, spatial response differences and
detector nonlinearity can also produce a trend. The ratio is diagnostic, not
a unique attribution to one mechanism. Common multiplicative cancellation
requires the assumptions above. No spatial shutter model is introduced.

No uncertainty or statistical weight is fabricated. Normalized points share
$R_{\rm ref}$ and therefore are correlated. This version records uncertainty
as unavailable; quantitative inference needs an explicit error/covariance model.

\section*{Connection to software}
\texttt{pycavi\_roi\_ratio.compute} implements matching, reference selection and
the equations as a pure function. It retains deep copies of both original
samples, units, availability, settings and membership. It uses finite checks
and a stable summation for the reference.

\texttt{pycavi\_roi\_ratio\_qc.publish} reads saved residual-ASDF original rows,
freezes the source graph locally, and writes a new immutable QC product using
the unchanged common factory, writer and reader. The operation is
\texttt{diagnostic.same\_exposure\_roi\_ratio}. It records source record digests,
byte hashes, producer revision and processing stage. Roundtrip equality and
source preservation are required. Broken links or identity mismatches fail.

\texttt{tools/compute-roi-ratios.py} consumes a completed source index and
writes fresh per-detector products plus a completion index. Expected real
coverage is 32 detectors, 20 matched exposures each: 640 ratio rows.
\texttt{tools/view-pair-qc.py --ratios} validates and embeds those products;
HTML only selects stored values. Its axes/cursor use the shared inspection
module. No scientific division, normalization or line fitting occurs there.

\section*{Verification}
Eight storage/computation tests cover analytic ratios and reference membership,
common-factor cancellation, exact member identity, unavailable cases, malformed
populations, relocation, frozen links, immutable sources and separate
CLI completion. Viewer checks disable both numerical services while loading
saved products. JavaScript checks exercise ratio modes, exact source inspection
and shared axes/cursor hooks. Actual Mac qualification and real-data ratio
publication are separate acceptance gates; fixture success is not scientific
validation of the detector or a shutter model.

differ, and relocated readers work without images or original native tables.
